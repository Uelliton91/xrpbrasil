Beginner's guide to XRP and XRPL

This guide is for readers who want to understand XRP and the XRP Ledger (XRPL) with clarity, without quick-profit promises and without confusing jargon. The goal is to take you from zero to a practical understanding of the technology.

If you want the fastest route, start here and then continue with How XRPL works and the current featured article.

By the end of this page, you should understand:

  • what XRP is and what it was designed to do;
  • how XRPL works underneath the surface;
  • how to study and test the network without buying anything first;
  • what the main security risks are and how to avoid them;
  • where to go next if you want a deeper understanding.

What is XRP?

XRP is a utility-focused digital asset used for settlement and value transfer inside XRPL. Unlike currencies created through mining, the entire supply of 100 billion XRP was defined at the genesis of the ledger.

In practice, XRP is used to:

  • pay transaction fees on XRPL, always as a very small amount that gets burned;
  • act as a bridge asset between different currencies and issued assets;
  • maintain a base reserve in each account, which helps prevent spam and abuse;
  • serve as a liquidity asset in products that rely on XRPL for fast settlement.

A meaningful share of supply remains in on-chain escrow with public rules, while the circulating supply is distributed across users, exchanges, and institutions.

XRPL in one view

XRPL is a public, permissionless payments network that closes a new ledger roughly every 3 to 5 seconds. It was built to be:

  • fast, with confirmations in seconds;
  • low-cost, with tiny transaction fees;
  • feature-rich, with native DEX, AMM, escrow, and NFT support;
  • open enough for anyone to run infrastructure and participate in the ecosystem.

How to start safely

A safe way to learn is by using the testnet, where you can receive test XRP from a faucet and explore the network without financial risk.

  1. Open the official faucet resources on xrpl.org and create a test account.
  2. Copy the address and inspect it on XRPScan to see balances and transactions.
  3. Use the available developer tools to send a simple transaction, such as a payment to another test account.
  4. If you write code, explore the official client libraries such as xrpl.js or xrpl-py.

The point is not to memorize every concept immediately. It is to build intuition safely before touching real funds.

Security first

Before dealing with real value, it is essential to internalize a few principles:

  • Never share your seed phrase or private keys, including screenshots or photos.
  • Use two-factor authentication wherever it is available, especially on exchanges, wallets, and email accounts.
  • Always verify the domain name and browser extensions you use. Many scams imitate legitimate websites closely.
  • Be skeptical of miracle airdrops, giveaways that ask for funds upfront, or any promise of guaranteed returns.
  • This site is educational. We do not request deposits, collect funds, or send private messages asking for money.

The most common mistakes in crypto are operational, not theoretical. Good security habits matter more than speed.

Wallets and custody

A wallet is the software or device that manages your private keys. On XRPL, the key controls the account. Whoever controls the key controls the balance.

  • Self-custody: you hold your own keys, such as on a hardware wallet or a non-custodial mobile wallet. More responsibility, more control.
  • Third-party custody: a company holds the keys for you, such as an exchange or institutional custodian. More convenience, but it requires trust.

A reasonable beginner path is:

  1. learn on the testnet with no real money;
  2. study custody models carefully before making any move;
  3. only then decide whether self-custody, a hardware wallet, or another setup makes sense for you.
