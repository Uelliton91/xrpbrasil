XRPL explained: how the XRP Ledger works

The XRP Ledger (XRPL) is a public, permissionless payments network with settlement in roughly 3 to 5 seconds. It was built to function as a global payments ledger with native exchange, asset issuance, AMM, and NFT capabilities.

If you want a broader starting point first, read the beginner's guide. This page is a technical map of the main building blocks.

It does not replace official documentation, but it helps frame the moving parts before you dive into code, infrastructure, or real-world integrations.

Accounts and keys

Every XRPL account has an address (usually starting with r...) and is controlled by one or more cryptographic keys. Each account also needs to maintain a minimum XRP reserve, which helps prevent spam.

  • Master key: can sign any transaction and can later be disabled in favor of another key setup.
  • Regular key: an alternate key for day-to-day usage, reducing exposure of the master key.
  • Multi-signing: allows a transaction to require multiple signatures, such as 2 of 3.

Transactions

Transactions are structured objects sent to the network. A few fields matter a lot:

  • Sequence: the account counter; each transaction consumes the next sequence number.
  • Fee: a small amount of XRP that gets burned.
  • LastLedgerSequence: the last ledger in which the transaction can be included before it expires.

A transaction becomes confirmed when the ledger that includes it is validated by consensus.

Native DEX and AMM

XRPL has had an on-ledger DEX since 2012. It is based on order books for each asset pair, and those orders live directly in the ledger itself.

On top of that, the protocol now includes a native AMM. Instead of depending on complex external smart contracts, the AMM follows protocol-level rules that allow users to:

  • create liquidity pools for asset pairs;
  • receive LP tokens when providing liquidity;
  • earn fees according to their participation;
  • combine AMM routes with the traditional DEX.

Issued assets and trustlines

Besides XRP, XRPL allows issuers to create assets representing fiat currencies, stablecoins, credits, or real-world assets. These are often called issued currencies or IOUs.

  • Issuer: the account that creates the asset, such as USD, EUR, or RLUSD.
  • Trustline: the trust relationship between recipient and issuer, including limits and rippling settings.
  • Rippling: a feature that allows IOU balances to flow across connected trust relationships; it can be disabled when needed.

In practice, you only receive an issued asset if you have a trustline for that issuer. Trust is local, explicit, and configurable.

Pathfinding and payments

The network's pathfinding engine can discover liquidity routes between two points by combining:

  • AMM pools;
  • DEX order books;
  • IOUs from different issuers;
  • XRP itself as a bridge asset.

That means a payment can begin in one issued asset and end in another, while the ledger automatically chooses the best route available at that moment.

Consensus and validators

XRPL uses its own consensus mechanism built around independent validators. Each server operator chooses a Unique Node List (UNL), which is the set of validators they trust for consensus participation.

  • Validators propose transaction sets for the next ledger.
  • When 80% or more of the UNL agrees, the ledger is considered validated.
  • Protocol changes move through the amendments process, where validators vote over time.

This architecture attempts to balance decentralization, performance, and security while avoiding proof-of-work mining.

FAQ

Common questions

Frequent questions about how XRPL actually works.

Is XRPL the same thing as Ripple?

No. XRPL is the public network. Ripple is a company that builds products and contributes to the ecosystem.

Are the fees expensive?

No. Fees on XRPL are typically very low and are paid in XRP.

Do I need XRP to use the network?

Yes. Every account must hold a small XRP reserve in order to exist on the ledger.
